What the material suggests

More than an aesthetic complaint

“Uncanny” explains the near-real mismatch. Food adds something sharper: a learned need to spot contamination, decay, and false promises before taking a bite.

Why businesses use it
Speed and lower production costs make generated advertising tempting, especially under thin restaurant margins.

Why audiences recoil
Wrong textures, repeated forms, and impossible anatomy collide with expectations of what food should safely look like.

Why trust matters
A photograph can be styled, but it still implies a dish existed. A generated image may advertise food that never did.
